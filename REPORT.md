## Research question and scope

The assignment asks for a GPT-2-style next-item recommender, a history-plus-item response model, reward-model-based RL, relevance and collapse/repeat evaluation, multi-step behavior discussion, and reproducible settings and limitations. It explicitly allows a negative RL result and says a generated recommendation does not imply user engagement. This project uses offline Yambda listen logs to test those components. It cannot measure causal response to an unexposed recommendation, retention, or satisfaction.

The immediate anchor is next-item prediction and conditional listen completion. A recurrent continuation-Q slate experiment is a later, bounded mechanism diagnostic proposed in the earlier AI note; it does not replace the assignment’s end-to-end anchor. The note’s A/B/C ideas are optional hypotheses. Nothing here treats them as established methods or results.

## Data and evaluation protocol

We use the pinned Yambda-50M listen stream (46,467,212 events, 9,238 users). The source uses five-second timestamp bins. The temporal benchmark split has 30-minute purge gaps and one-day validation and test windows. For each target at time `t`, the user history contains only events with timestamp strictly less than `t`. Events tied at `t` share the same history; within-tie order is not observed. Primary training and evaluation are event-level, one target per event, with equal event weighting.

The output catalog contains the 100,000 most frequent training-period items. The persisted legacy model class map is not popularity order. In-catalog history items use their frozen class; out-of-catalog history events remain in the sequence as `UNK`. Unsupported held-out targets are never inserted into the candidate set and remain in the all-target metric denominator as misses. We report organic and recommendation-driven listens separately because their ranking behavior differs substantially. Previously seen items remain eligible; repeat behavior is measured rather than ruled out.

The response label is clipped played fraction, conditional on an observed listen. The dataset has no impression/no-listen rows or logged action propensities. Accordingly, RM accuracy is predictive, not a counterfactual response estimate; RL reward and slate utility are proxy quantities. We make no OPE or causal retention claim. Aggregate test schema/support/outcome QA was inspected during initial profiling. A later source audit transiently loaded item-token and duration features in chunks before masking to training rows; it did not read `played_ratio_pct` labels, model predictions, RM scores, or performance in that scan. Two failed ranking preflights streamed test-index file bytes for SHA-256 only. R9 ranking outcomes were then scored under frozen R6 selection without retuning. A separate RM cohort was frozen and scored only as observed-listen prediction; its results did not feed ranking-policy choices. The C R4 attempt later opened event arrays and sampled test indices but failed before policy scores or test metrics were produced; this exposure is recorded in the C run log and is distinct from the pre-array R2/R3 failures. The incomplete seed17 full-supported GPT replication and dependent gate/grid remain resource omissions, not negative evidence. The cohort-scoped selection and prior exposure are linked in [`research/preregistrations/test-freeze-cohort-scope-20261019.json`](research/preregistrations/test-freeze-cohort-scope-20261019.json), [`research/results/test-index-byte-integrity-exposure-20261019.json`](research/results/test-index-byte-integrity-exposure-20261019.json), [`research/results/core-ranking-test-results-20261020.md`](research/results/core-ranking-test-results-20261020.md), and [`research/results/rm-test-results-20261020.md`](research/results/rm-test-results-20261020.md).

## One-step RL and exact reward tilt

One-step REINFORCE used a frozen base policy, frozen combined RM, temperature-one categorical actions, full-catalog forward KL to the base, and prespecified KL coefficients 0.01 and 0.1. The CE and RL arms had the same 2,000-update training budget and sampled training-context exposure. The validation-selected CE checkpoint is step 2,000 (NDCG@100/Recall@100 0.06351/0.1969); each RL checkpoint is step 500 (β=.01: 0.05959/0.1881; β=.1: 0.06013/0.1911), after both RL curves declined by step 2,000. Thus the best-checkpoint comparison matches the training budget and selection procedure, but the selected weights have different update exposure. The final-step CE-versus-RL comparison uses step 2,000 for all arms and matches update exposure exactly. Neither comparison is FLOP-matched because RL also evaluates the reference, full KL, and reward model. This is a short optimization envelope, not a converged-policy comparison.

An exact full-catalog H1 calculation separates the target from the learned optimizer. For a frozen RM and reference policy, the exact target is `qβ(i|h) ∝ π₀(i|h) exp(r̂(h,i)/β)`. At target β=.01, this distribution raises predicted RM score but sharply reduces validation relevance (NDCG@100 0.03140 vs base 0.06346 on the 1,024-query diagnostic subset). At target β=.1, NDCG@100 is 0.06128, near the base. The learned actors are not the exact q distributions: actor-to-q KL and objective gaps are reported separately by **training β** and **target β** in [`research/results/rl-envelope-and-exact-h1.md`](research/results/rl-envelope-and-exact-h1.md). Thus the low-temperature finding reflects the proxy target’s reward/relevance tradeoff; optimizer gaps are a distinct issue. Neither result measures user utility.

CRN sampled-action evaluations show small positive frozen-RM score shifts for RL and the fixed mixture. They are estimates over 16 sampled actions per context and do not include sampled item IDs in the preserved archive, so item exposure concentration and repeat-rate comparisons cannot be inferred from them. These diagnostics do not replace ranking evaluation. A finite-16 variance decomposition finds that between-context variance dominates scalar combined-RM score variance (about 97% under the seed-43 base). The actual frozen history-RM baseline gives direct finite-16 centered-score MSE .00548, versus .02869 for the fixed .6514 baseline; the separate ideal context-mean variance estimate is .00077 on the same sampled actions. These are reward-score calculations, not policy-gradient variance results, because the gradient also depends on action log-probabilities. One bounded context-baseline control is planned with the reward target unchanged. See [`research/results/crn16-reward-variance.md`](research/results/crn16-reward-variance.md).

## Bounded recurrent continuation-Q diagnostic

The proposed C branch fixes observed history and constructs a separate generated slate. It uses a 16-item shared pool (top 8 GPT plus top 8 recent-frequency items, with deterministic overlap fill), horizon 4, a Plackett–Luce reference without replacement, and terminal utility equal to mean frozen combined-RM score minus a fixed positive-cosine diversity penalty. The logged target is not injected. Validation pool coverage is only 9.96% overall (19.15% organic, 2.17% recommended), so conclusions are limited to this deliberately narrow pool and constructed utility.

Exact dynamic programming provides a finite-pool target and controls. On 512 validation contexts at β=.01, exact q has mean raw proxy utility 0.65030 and regularized objective 0.64509; the reference has 0.63975 for both. A statewise myopic reference tilt using the exact marginal utility is close in regularized objective (0.64435; shortfall 0.00074). Incremental-utility greedy and exact-Q argmax attain higher raw proxy utility (about 0.680) but incur large KL and lower regularized objective. The maximum raw-utility set is a raw-utility upper bound, not the KL-regularized optimum. These computations validate the finite-DAG objective and reveal a strong simple control; they do not show learned recurrence improves recommendation.

The qteacher_R1 capacity fit completed 2,000×256 replay updates and evaluation on all 512 validation contexts: Q MAE .003502, regularized J .644590 versus exact q .645086 and myopic .644345. Paired user-cluster intervals show learned-minus-myopic J +.000245 [+.000209,+.000285] and learned-minus-exact J −.000495 [−.000543,−.000449]. The learned R1 is near exact q in the fixed proxy and has slightly higher J than myopic, though its raw proxy utility is .000960 lower; this is not evidence of a recurrence benefit. A posthoc finite-DAG audit of all legal edges and 512 contexts found the uniform `2 Σ ε_t` bound .229681 covered the largest observed objective gap .006181 in all contexts; this is a finite-state approximation guarantee and is loose. Results and limits are in [`research/results/continuation-q-capacity.md`](research/results/continuation-q-capacity.md).

The seven-arm seed-17 run set has full-512 validation exports and a 10,000-replicate paired user-cluster bootstrap. The seed-29 pair completed the same fixed updates; its intervals are separate from seed17. Validation contrasts and training limits are in [`research/results/continuation-q-runset-validation.md`](research/results/continuation-q-runset-validation.md). R5 evaluated all nine fixed step-2,000 models on 512 label-blind contexts. The preregistered primary SubTB shared R4−R1 contrast was +.0001812 in J (95% user-cluster CI [.0001593,.0002030]); seed29 repeated the direction at +.0000871 ([.0000743,.0000999]). Both reduced all-edge Q MAE while increasing KL. The gain is not unique to SubTB: qteacher and joint R4 controls also improve slightly over R1. Untied R4 is .0000512 above shared R4 in point J; that direct contrast was not registered and has no paired interval. Exact-Q J=.663118; SubTB R1/shared/untied remain .000514/.000333/.000282 below it. Exact-Q versus myopic headroom is .000624 in this utility and pool, so the observed model-approximation gain is only part of the potential planning gain. Test-target coverage is 65/512 (12.7%) overall, 59/243 (24.3%) organic, and 6/269 (2.2%) recommendation-driven. This is a finite-pool proxy result, not catalog-wide relevance or user utility. ## Integrated evidence and current decision

The assignment's sequence remains GPT next-item ranking, conditional reward modeling, then reward-guided RL. Ranking evidence includes a strong frozen full-supported seed-43 result and positive frozen gate-versus-global-mixture test contrasts on this cohort, especially for recommendation-driven events; the full-supported-versus-sampled SFT comparison changes exposure and is not a data-only effect. The matched continuation narrows that question: with one common pretrained parent and equal nominal exposure, full-supported minus repeated-subset validation NDCG@100 was +.000447 (95% paired UID CI [−.001641,+.002698]). Both arms improved over the common parent, but this pair neither shows a clear breadth advantage nor explains the earlier from-scratch difference. See the [R5 matched-continuation note](research/results/matched-data-continuation-r5-validation.md) and [frozen ranking results](research/results/core-ranking-test-results-20261020.md).

The reward models predict logged completion conditional on an observed listen; that signal does not measure response to a hypothetical recommendation. The grouped-RL recovery gives a clearer objective-versus-ranking split: RLOO-8 reduced the frozen exact-q gap versus P0 on the 878 supported queries, while both policy-gradient actors ranked below matched CE on the separate 10k full-catalog sample. This is declared proxy-objective optimization, not user utility or policy-class optimality; see the detailed results and cohort limits above. The C=16 model gains are small against similar controls and a .000624 exact-Q/myopic objective gap; low target coverage, particularly on recommended events, limits the practical interpretation. Wider pools increased coverage in the validation diagnostic without increasing estimated planning headroom. ## Multi-step and long-term outcomes

The assignment’s multi-step discussion must distinguish observed events from synthetic recommendations. Appending a generated item as if it had been clicked/listened to creates a simulated feedback loop, not an observation. The proposed C slate keeps the observed history fixed for this reason. A five-step autonomous rollout and ground-truth-history comparison are **not yet reported**. Even if run, they would measure distribution drift and repeat behavior under declared synthetic transitions, not real long-term engagement. Estimating retention or satisfaction would require impression/exposure logs, outcomes for non-clicked recommendations, and an appropriate prospective or counterfactual design.
